# African Music Industry Brief: Royalties, Live Bottlenecks, and Tech Solutions

## Editorial

Copyright collection and cross-border live circuits are facing structural pressure across the continent this week. In South Africa, uncollected publishing royalties highlight the steep price artists pay for missing registrations, while political friction between Sahelian states and Côte d'Ivoire is directly disrupting tour routes. At the same time, regional market infrastructure continues to mature, from local automated audio engineering platforms in Dakar to new B2B export markets in Abuja. The gap between creative output and administrative rigor remains the primary barrier to sustainable music revenue in Africa.

## Top stories

**South African mechanicals risk redistribution without prompt registration.** The Composers, Authors and Publishers Association of South Africa (CAPASSO) collected 269 million South African rand over the past year. Under its operating rules, digital service provider mechanical payments not claimed within a three-year suspense account are distributed to other registered songwriters, underscoring the urgency for composers to register works before CAPASSO's December payout, as detailed by [The InterSpace Daily](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/capasso-south-africa-mechanical-royalties-streaming-three-year-suspense/).

**Regional political friction disrupts live circuits between the Sahel and Côte d'Ivoire.** Diplomatic tensions between Côte d'Ivoire and the military-led governments of the Alliance of Sahel States (Burkina Faso, Mali, Niger) are spilling onto the stage. Statements by reggae icon Alpha Blondy in Abidjan ignited online blowback, following the mid-October cancellation of Burkinabe artist Issouf Manem-Yam's concert in the Ivorian economic capital, reports [Orange Actualités](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMi5AFBVV95cUxNbnR6cl9xNnM1N3Boa19OS1I5aUtNLV8wZWFILS1uNk9LZEhzT0lRTzZiWFpJRnc2azhWWGdMdHJEVi1ZYVAxeHlfV1JyVFVHckVDb2lvLUl6UVdMVmlkakJLTHZVUkxCMjFSOFRNNWtTQnlXanJGRTFRdDYxc3hkU3hYTEtBc2ZpWV9oUUZVcVUwa0RrRW45anNJYlY2Nm9BZUxlQ09DQmZRY2hNSVhSQ2k1Znd6RkJzX0Y2dTBkbnlIX2NkWjRIdnY0Zk8tU3VDVWw1RWdTNjdDd29qSUUwbHZTYl8?oc=5).

**Sen Mixmaster launches African-built audio mastering platform in Dakar.** Founded by CEO Boubacar Djiba, the service aims to provide low-cost, high-caliber audio mastering using calibrated open-source models without generative artificial intelligence, deleting user uploads post-processing to protect master rights. The tool was unveiled alongside a preview of rapper One Lyrical's upcoming project, according to [Le Soleil](https://lesoleil.sn/actualites/arts-et-culture/sen-mixmaster-une-plateforme-africaine-pour-laccessibilite-et-la-qualite-sonore/).

## Rights and regulation

**Liberian blank levy distributed as welfare instead of airplay royalties.** Liberia's 2016 Intellectual Property Act established a levy on recording devices intended to fund creator rights. However, the Collective Societies of Liberia (COSOL) has distributed these proceeds as general welfare payments rather than title-by-title airplay royalties, leaving the statutory 70/20/10 split between performers, producers, and administration unapplied, per [The InterSpace Daily](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/liberia-cosol-copyright-levy-welfare-performers-airplay-royalties/).

**WOMA-R to tackle Afrobeats behind-the-scenes remuneration.** The Wale Olomu Memorial Awards and Roundtable will examine producer and songwriter compensation on December 12 during a broadcast on TopRadio 90.9 FM in Lagos, ahead of an industry awards gala on December 19, reports [QED.NG](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMilwFBVV95cUxNaWluZUFpN3R0STFyUzlyaHQ2UTV0N3h1UldYcDc1cXd6ZXl1SDZGb05YaUlhOEtBeDRqN3lvRUhYRUdNYi05OGJwaHcyYkktZkpyVl9JUlRkYlF6QU9QZVRPai10ZHdvMG54ZzhSY1JSaUl5Ui1raVpGbVFrTzg2N1VUWHBLT3dRaENJeHFuVC04RjBfTUhz?oc=5).

**ARIPO holds copyright symposium in Kampala.** In an announcement from the organization, the [African Regional Intellectual Property Organization](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMitAFBVV95cUxNcThEaHpXVXdUclVkQmxuNGRhR3BFMXF6QXN5WjF6OU12bVJUaWpXNnltUzNQa09CWWFCMTl5UHoyT2pSWXRSRlU4WmVRYTZGSC12aHN2Ti1GNHp2TlpXb2xMU0NxZkVPWVByX2dtOFNKM0FSai1QZEFPaTdUeHBlTmNGQ1VVRXlUdGlON3lMSjQzNlIyTFlMaTlCWnJaY2tqT2x5WGtjSS1TQk5VeVFSQmJQYmk?oc=5) marked its 50th anniversary by gathering regional stakeholders to discuss remuneration structures across member states.

## Key takeaways

- **Audit South African mechanical collection:** Songwriters and publishers with South African streaming volume must verify song registrations with CAPASSO before December to prevent unclaimed royalties from expiring into the three-year suspense pool.
- **Factor geopolitical volatility into West African touring:** Booking artists between Côte d'Ivoire and Sahelian nations (Burkina Faso, Mali, Niger) requires monitoring regional political tensions to avoid short-notice cancellations and boycott campaigns.
- **Calendar around religious observances early:** Sauti za Busara's shift to March 2027 shows that live event producers in East and North Africa should align booking cycles and marketing timelines well ahead of the Ramadan calendar.
- **Check YouTube monetization outside Senegal:** According to Universal Music Africa, YouTube monetizes music content only in Senegal for now, so rights holders in other French-speaking markets may want to verify their catalog's status.
