The Payout Gap in Benin and the Rise of Francophone Live Power

Editorial

This week highlights the persistent gap between legislation and revenue distribution, notably with Benin's decade-old private copy levy still lacking a functioning payout route. Meanwhile, the live sector shows massive potential as Fally Ipupa sets new records for Francophone music in London. Professionalizing registration remains the priority for the industry, from Morocco’s triple-entry requirements to Audiomack’s strict enrollment rules.

Top stories

Benin's Private Copy Levy Stalled: Despite a 2015 decree establishing a private copy levy, a recent study by CISAC (International Confederation of Societies of Authors and Composers) shows no functioning payout chain for rights holders. Artists and labels still lack the necessary receipts and distribution rules to access this income. The InterSpace Daily.

Fally Ipupa's London Record: The Congolese star's two-night run at The O2 in October marks the largest Francophone concert in UK history. The venue reports that 2026 is its most linguistically diverse year, with 18 languages represented on stage. IQ Magazine.

Morocco's Registration Complexity: Royalties in Morocco require three separate registrations with BMDAV (Bureau Marocain du Droit d'Auteur et des Droits Voisins). An ISRC (International Standard Recording Code) only identifies the recording, meaning the song, the performers, and the producer must be registered individually to reconcile all claims. The InterSpace Daily.

Business and sponsorship

Olamide Partners with Jeroid: Nigerian rapper and entrepreneur Olamide has signed as a brand ambassador for Jeroid, a Nigerian fintech and digital asset trading platform. The partnership aims to support the company’s global expansion plans. TechCabal.

Cool FM Launches Music Awards: The Nigerian radio station announced the COMA music awards featuring 17 categories for artists and behind-the-scenes creatives. Winners will be determined through a combination of performance data, judging, and audience participation. The InterSpace Daily.

Sterling Bank Renews Aké Festival Support: Sterling Bank has extended its partnership with the Aké Arts and Book Festival in Lagos. The 14th edition will explore "Native Intelligence," examining how African languages and cultural experiences are represented in artificial intelligence. QED.NG.

Streaming and platforms

Audiomack AMP Enrollment Rules: The Audiomack Monetization Program (AMP) does not offer back-pay for streams accumulated before a creator enrolls and activates their uploads. The platform uses a 50% revenue-share formula with a net-90 payment schedule. The InterSpace Daily.

Gospel Platform in DRC: A new digital initiative, Gospel Talents du Grand Katanga, has launched to promote young religious artists in the Democratic Republic of Congo. The platform uses YouTube, Facebook, and WhatsApp to distribute video performances and facilitate audience voting. ACP.

Nigeria Chart Moves: Olamide's "Burukutu" debuted at No. 9 on the TurnTable Official Nigeria Top 100, showing stronger performance on radio than on streaming in its first week. Meanwhile, BNXN's "If You Like" reached No. 3 on Apple Music Nigeria. The InterSpace Daily.

Live and festivals

Shakira Postpones Egypt Show: The concert at the Pyramids of Giza, originally set for November 28, has been moved to April 2, 2027. This is the second postponement for the date, with the artist citing regional circumstances. Egyptian Streets.

Thione Seck Tribute in Senegal: Baaba Maal and Wally Seck will perform a joint tribute to the late Thione Seck during the "Les Blues du fleuve" festival. The event is scheduled for December 4 to 6 in Podor and Ndioum. SenTV.

Johnnie Walker AfroExchange: The brand's cultural platform hosted Ohangla star Prince Indah in Mombasa. The event is part of a series designed to facilitate musical exchange between different Kenyan regions. Business Today Kenya.

Rights and regulation

Copyright Damages in Uganda: The High Court Commercial Division in Kampala ordered the estate of the late Justice George Kanyeihamba to pay 45 million Ugandan Shillings (approximately 12,000 USD) for copyright theft. The court found that the late judge used a researcher's original work in a law textbook without authorization. Daily Monitor Uganda.

Other African news

Gringo Festival Returns: Zimbabwe will host the third edition of the Gringo Festival on November 7 in Mabvuku, featuring a soccer tournament and a musical gala in honor of the late actor Lazarus Boora. allAfrica.

Seggae Festival in Mauritius: The Festival Seggae Zwe takes place this Saturday at Stade Anjalay, featuring 17 local artists to celebrate the island's musical heritage. allAfrica.

Solidarity for Bekoto: In Madagascar, artists including Rolf and Lemanana organized a concert to raise funds for the medical treatment and potential evacuation of Bekoto, a member of the group Mahaleo. allAfrica.

Key takeaways

  1. Activate monetization early: Platforms like Audiomack do not pay retroactively, so labels must ensure AMP enrollment is active before a release gains traction.
  2. Verify regional registration rules: In markets like Morocco, an ISRC is insufficient for full royalty collection; separate filings for authors, performers, and producers are mandatory.
  3. Monitor Francophone live demand: The record-breaking success of Fally Ipupa in London indicates a significant opportunity for Francophone African artists to headline major European arenas.
