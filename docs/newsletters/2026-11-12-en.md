# African Music Industry Dispatch: Unlocking Revenue Potential and End-of-Year Live Surge

## Editorial

Research presented at CANEX 2026 reveals that stronger rights administration could unlock nearly $300 million annually across key African markets like Nigeria and Kenya. While structural gaps in royalty collection persist, commercial actors are building alternative monetization paths, from local telecom integrations to brand-backed artist development programmes. As the year-end live season approaches, label anniversaries, curated reunions, and major festival bookings across the continent demonstrate strong consumer demand for live music experiences.

## Top stories

- **Copyright reform could unlock $300 million annually in West and East Africa**: Research by the Music Economy Development Initiative (MEDI) presented at the CANEX Intellectual Property Rights Summit in Lagos indicates that Nigeria could generate $230 million and Kenya $55 million annually if copyright collection frameworks operated efficiently. Speaking at the event, Angela Ndambuki, Regional Director for Sub-Saharan Africa at the International Federation of the Phonographic Industry (IFPI), stressed that weak administration and inadequate enforcement continue to prevent creators from receiving proper revenue. [Downtown Music Africa](https://downtownmusic.africa/africas-music-industry-could-unlock-nearly-300-million-in-annual-revenue-through-stronger-rights-management/), [Music In Africa](https://musicinafrica.net/magazine/ifpis-angela-ndambuki-urges-africa-to-unlock-music-copyright-revenue/)
- **Tusker partners with Bien for album launch and mentorship initiative**: Kenya Breweries Limited brand Tusker hosted a listening event for Kenyan musician Bien ahead of his upcoming album *Alusa Continua*, scheduled for release on November 20. Through their joint Base to Billboardz mentorship initiative, Tusker and Bien selected six emerging Kenyan artists, Muringi Matheri, Manasseh Shalom, Zawadi, Chris Barr, Njuguna, and Ras Amor, to receive professional development and industry exposure. [Business Today Kenya](https://businesstoday.co.ke/tusker-partners-with-bien-ahead-of-album/)
- **CREAM and Cinq Music launch USSD music purchasing in Nigeria**: Creative Reality Entertainment Art and Music (CREAM), the Nigerian direct-to-fan platform founded by D’banj, has partnered with US-based Cinq Music Group to distribute international recordings locally. The agreement enables Nigerian consumers to purchase catalog tracks, such as T.I.’s "Live Your Life" featuring Rihanna, directly via USSD codes, full downloads, and caller ring-back tones priced in local currency. [BusinessDay Nigeria](https://businessday.ng/life-arts/article/cream-partners-cinq-music-to-bring-international-recordings-to-nigerian-fans/)

## Key takeaways

- **Verify creator metadata identifiers prior to distribution**: Ensure IPI numbers from rights organizations (such as GHAMRO or CAPASSO) are registered alongside ISWC and ISRC codes before release to avoid metadata mismatches and unallocated royalties.
- **Utilize USSD and carrier billing for local monetization**: Digital service providers and rights holders targeting African markets can bypass low credit card penetration by structuring direct-to-fan sales via telecom partnerships and local USSD channels.
- **Structure brand partnerships around talent incubators**: Corporate sponsorships can extend beyond event branding by incorporating structured artist development programmes that build long-term catalog value.
