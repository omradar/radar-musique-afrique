African Music Industry Brief: Royalties, Live Bottlenecks, and Tech Solutions

Editorial

Copyright collection and cross-border live circuits are facing structural pressure across the continent this week. In South Africa, uncollected publishing royalties highlight the steep price artists pay for missing registrations, while political friction between Sahelian states and Côte d'Ivoire is directly disrupting tour routes. At the same time, regional market infrastructure continues to mature, from local automated audio engineering platforms in Dakar to new B2B export markets in Abuja. The gap between creative output and administrative rigor remains the primary barrier to sustainable music revenue in Africa.

Top stories

South African mechanicals risk redistribution without prompt registration. The Composers, Authors and Publishers Association of South Africa (CAPASSO) collected 269 million South African rand over the past year. Under its operating rules, digital service provider mechanical payments not claimed within a three-year suspense account are distributed to other registered songwriters, underscoring the urgency for composers to register works before CAPASSO's December payout, as detailed by The InterSpace Daily.

Regional political friction disrupts live circuits between the Sahel and Côte d'Ivoire. Diplomatic tensions between Côte d'Ivoire and the military-led governments of the Alliance of Sahel States (Burkina Faso, Mali, Niger) are spilling onto the stage. Statements by reggae icon Alpha Blondy in Abidjan ignited online blowback, following the mid-October cancellation of Burkinabe artist Issouf Manem-Yam's concert in the Ivorian economic capital, reports Orange Actualités.

Sen Mixmaster launches African-built audio mastering platform in Dakar. Founded by CEO Boubacar Djiba, the service aims to provide low-cost, high-caliber audio mastering using calibrated open-source models without generative artificial intelligence, deleting user uploads post-processing to protect master rights. The tool was unveiled alongside a preview of rapper One Lyrical's upcoming project, according to Le Soleil.

Rights and regulation

Liberian blank levy distributed as welfare instead of airplay royalties. Liberia's 2016 Intellectual Property Act established a levy on recording devices intended to fund creator rights. However, the Collective Societies of Liberia (COSOL) has distributed these proceeds as general welfare payments rather than title-by-title airplay royalties, leaving the statutory 70/20/10 split between performers, producers, and administration unapplied, per The InterSpace Daily.

WOMA-R to tackle Afrobeats behind-the-scenes remuneration. The Wale Olomu Memorial Awards and Roundtable will examine producer and songwriter compensation on December 12 during a broadcast on TopRadio 90.9 FM in Lagos, ahead of an industry awards gala on December 19, reports QED.NG.

ARIPO holds copyright symposium in Kampala. In an announcement from the organization, the African Regional Intellectual Property Organization marked its 50th anniversary by gathering regional stakeholders to discuss remuneration structures across member states.

Business and sponsorship

Rwandan imprint Giti Inc. signs singer Chiboo. Label founder Junior Giti confirmed a five-year management agreement with the young vocalist, following the label's previous five-year artist development cycle with Chriss Eazy, reports The New Times Rwanda.

Rhythm And Revenue 3.0 masterclass targets career sustainability. The music business training initiative convened creative professionals on October 30 in Victoria Island, Lagos, under the direction of convener Theresa Umoh, to address long-term catalog funding and business models, according to WETALKSOUND.

Bell Lager drives ObaFest brand activation in Mbarara. The beverage brand brought its experiential live property to Kakyeeka Stadium in western Uganda on October 24, using a homecoming concert headlined by regional artist TPaul to anchor brand engagement, reports allAfrica.

Streaming and platforms

Wizkid crosses 12 billion total Spotify streams. The Nigerian artist became the first African act to reach 12 billion streams on Spotify across all credited features and lead roles, according to Arise News. Separate commercial reporting from Legit News notes his studio album Morayo logged a record 12.12 million first-day streams on Spotify Nigeria.

Rema logs 1.2 billion Spotify streams in 2026. The singer has generated over 1.2 billion streams on the platform since January 2026, pushing his cumulative career catalog beyond 6.8 billion streams across lead and guest appearances, reports Arise News.

EMPIRE acts dominate Nigerian digital and broadcast rankings. Independent distributor EMPIRE secured heavy placement via FOLA and Seyi Vibez, who together occupied 8 of the top 15 slots on Spotify Nigeria and 7 of the top 10 on TurnTable Charts (Nigeria's official chart provider), as tracked by The InterSpace Daily.

Live and festivals

Sauti za Busara shifts 2027 festival to March. Organizers of the Zanzibar showcase festival announced that its 24th edition will take place from March 19 to 21, 2027, moving from its traditional February window to accommodate Ramadan. The line-up features acts including Dulla Makabila, Yugen Blakrok, and Isabel Novella, per allAfrica.

Abuja Afrojazz Festival launches international music market. The festival's fourth edition (November 18 to 21, 2026) will feature a dedicated B2B market on November 19 and 20 in Jabi, backed by the Institut français and the European Union Delegation. The platform connects 20 international delegates from 10 countries with local artists, alongside performances headlined by Cobhams Asuquo, according to allAfrica.

Milk + Cookies brings Olivia Dean to South Africa. The festival confirmed British singer Olivia Dean will headline events at Cape Town's Sandringham Farm on January 29 and Johannesburg's Expo Centre on January 31, joining local acts Elaine and Zee Nxumalo, per IQ Magazine.

Baaba Maal and Wally Seck announce joint tribute to Thione Seck. Production entity Marva Music confirmed the two Senegalese stars will perform a collaborative set dedicated to Thione Seck's catalog at the Les Blues du fleuve festival in Podor and Ndioum, running from December 4 to 6, 2026, reports SenTV.

Omah Lay pauses Clarity of Mind tour. The Nigerian artist has postponed all live dates starting October 30 for four to six weeks to recover from a knee injury, with revised dates and ticket terms pending, reports The InterSpace Daily.

Bagamoyo Festival gathers 146 applicant troupes. The 44th International Festival of Arts and Culture opened October 28 at the Tanzania Institute of Arts and Culture (TaSUBa) under a climate awareness theme, drawing applications from 104 Tanzanian groups and 42 foreign delegations, according to the Daily News Tanzania.

Other African news

Phyno releases 15-track album with MAD Solutions. The rapper's project Let That Sink In features seven guest artists, including Seyi Vibez, distributed through a partnership between Penthauze and MAD Solutions, reports The InterSpace Daily.

Peruzzi, Zlatan, and Shoday chart on release day. Their joint single INFERNO hit No. 6 on the Apple Music Nigeria chart on the evening of its October 29 release and appeared on local YouTube trending lists, per The InterSpace Daily.

Rajery and Raúl Rodríguez tour collaborative album in Spain. Malagasy valiha virtuoso Rajery and Spanish musician Raúl Rodríguez completed an Iberian promotional tour from October 20 to 26 for their joint record Mandry ve ?, including an appearance at the Barcelona Jazz Festival, according to allAfrica.

Key takeaways

  • Audit South African mechanical collection: Songwriters and publishers with South African streaming volume must verify song registrations with CAPASSO before December to prevent unclaimed royalties from expiring into the three-year suspense pool.
  • Factor geopolitical volatility into West African touring: Booking artists between Côte d'Ivoire and Sahelian nations (Burkina Faso, Mali, Niger) requires monitoring regional political tensions to avoid short-notice cancellations and boycott campaigns.
  • Calendar around religious observances early: Sauti za Busara's shift to March 2027 shows that live event producers in East and North Africa should align booking cycles and marketing timelines well ahead of the Ramadan calendar.
