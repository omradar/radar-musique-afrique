African Music Industry Weekly: Mechanical Royalties, Chart Dynamics, and Tour Adjustments

Editorial

Rights administration and live infrastructure take center stage this week. From mechanical royalty collection mechanisms in South Africa to structural distribution questions in Liberia, publishing registration remains an urgent operational priority. Meanwhile, platform distribution partnerships continue to consolidate presence on digital charts, while live tour schedules across the region face unexpected calendar adjustments.

Top stories

South Africa's CAPASSO reports R269 million collected in a year
Mechanical rights hub CAPASSO processes streaming royalties through a 12.5% fee structure and quarterly payouts. Streams for unregistered works move into a three-year suspense account, after which unallocated funds are distributed to other registered writers. Rights holders must register works prior to the December distribution cycle to secure their payouts. Source: The InterSpace Daily.

Liberian copyright levy administration under review
Liberia's 2016 IP Act mandates a levy on copying devices and grants performers half of airplay royalties. However, collective management organization COSOL has distributed these funds as welfare payments rather than per-song royalties. Performers and labels operate under distinct framework rules under the 70/20/10 split model. Source: The InterSpace Daily.

EMPIRE secures heavy presence on Nigerian charts
Artists FOLA and Seyi Vibez, both backed by distributor EMPIRE, appear on eight of the top 15 tracks on Spotify Nigeria and seven entries in the TurnTable top 10. Industry metrics show varying trends when comparing pure digital stream volume against traditional broadcast radio reach for these titles. Source: The InterSpace Daily.

Business and sponsorship

Universal Music Africa is prioritizing efforts to support more equitable monetization models across digital and catalog revenue streams, according to statements by Managing Director Franck-Alcide Kacou. Source: Stratégies.

Beverage brand Bell Lager sponsored the Obafest event experience in Mbarara, Uganda, centering its regional brand activation around artist Tpaul's homecoming performance. Source: allAfrica (EN).

Streaming and platforms

Chart data from Nigeria underscores contrasting consumption patterns across media formats. While high-volume digital releases dominate streaming platforms, traditional radio programming maintains distinct playlist dynamics that impact overall audience reach. Source: The InterSpace Daily.

Live and festivals

Omah Lay has postponed all remaining dates on his Clarity of Mind tour starting October 30 following a knee injury. The recovery period is estimated at four to six weeks, with replacement dates and refund terms yet to be published. Source: The InterSpace Daily.

Organizers for Sauti Za Busara in Zanzibar have announced their artist lineup and confirmed that the upcoming festival edition will move to March. Source: allAfrica (EN).

Milk + Cookies Festival has announced British singer Olivia Dean as a headline act for its 2027 edition at Sandringham Farm in Cape Town as the platform expands its live activity in South Africa. Source: Business Today Kenya.

Burkinabè singer Nabalüm marked ten years in music with a headline performance at the Salle des Banquets de Ouaga 2000 on October 24, 2026. Source: Burkina24.

Rights and regulation

In an official corporate announcement, intellectual property body ARIPO announced it is marking its 50th anniversary with a copyright symposium in Kampala addressing creator compensation across Africa. Source: ARIPO.

Key takeaways

  • Songwriters can avoid losing mechanical streaming income by ensuring catalog metadata is registered before CMO deadlines, such as CAPASSO's December cutoff.
  • Rightsholders operating across West Africa may need to verify whether local collection societies disburse blank media levies as direct per-song royalties or general welfare funds.
  • Tour management teams can protect live operations by defining explicit rescheduling and refund terms ahead of time to handle health-related delays.
