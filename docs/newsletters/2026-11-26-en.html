Africa Music Radar

Legal Battles, Live Infrastructure, and the Economic Engine of African Music

Editorial

This week highlights the gap between Africa’s creative reach and its underlying business framework. While Lagos prepares for another massive festive concert season and independent tech platforms emerge in North Africa, critical issues remain around contracts and royalty administration. In Kenya, a 87.6 million Kenyan shilling legal dispute exposes the ongoing friction between independent talent and label agreements. For executives and creators alike, building sustainable infrastructure, from local copyright enforcement to cross-border live payments, remains the central challenge.

Top stories

Fathermoh sues Black Market Records for 87.6 million Kenyan shillings
Kenyan Gengetone artist Moses Otieno, known as Fathermoh, has taken US-based Black Market Records to court, seeking 87.6 million Kenyan shillings (approximately $675,000) over alleged withheld royalties, catalog access restrictions, and unauthorized exploitation. Court filings show the 2021 agreement assigned production, marketing, and distribution to the label in exchange for 20% of royalties and 50% of publishing rights. This dispute follows a broader legal injunction reported by The EastAfrican, which barred the label from exploiting several Kenyan artists' catalogs, as detailed by Business Today Kenya.

Lagos creative economy: Nightlife hits $2 billion as venue gaps persist
A new report titled Off the Charts: The Afrobeats Export Economy, published by TAG by Oui Capital and Livespot360, values the Lagos nightlife economy at $2 billion in 2025, projecting it could reach $2.8 billion by 2027 with additional venues and recurring events. The study notes that Nigerian artists' Spotify payouts could climb from $39.4 million to $99.4 million if the share of international listeners increases, even without rising total streams, according to Vanguard News.

Live Nation signals expansion in Nigeria after Dave's record Lagos dates
British-Nigerian rapper Dave drew over 4,000 attendees across two nights at the newly refurbished ₦68 billion (€42 million) Wole Soyinka Centre for Culture and the Creative Arts in October, setting an attendance record for a single act at the venue. Promoted by Live Nation alongside local firm MASSIVE, the tour marks Live Nation's growing operational focus on West Africa ahead of the opening of the planned 12,000-capacity Lagos Arena, reports IQ Magazine.

Business and sponsorship

  • Aristokrat Records CEO reflects on Burna Boy renewal terms: Peedi Picasso, CEO of Aristokrat Records, revealed he once rejected an 85-15% revenue split demanded by Burna Boy during contract renewal talks, following an earlier 60-40 split in the singer's favor. Picasso stated that the economics of the Nigerian market at the time, which relied heavily on live dates and endorsements rather than recorded sales, made the demand unviable, as reported by Blueprint Newspapers.
  • Chocolate City combines physical scouting with AI in A&R: Osagie Onobun, head of A&R at Nigerian label Chocolate City, explained that alongside physical university tours in cities like Enugu and Ile-Ife, the company now systematically integrates digital discovery apps, artificial intelligence searches, and streaming audience analytics to sign new talent, according to Business News Nigeria.
  • Corporate talent programs and Afrobeats development: Sustained corporate competitions over the past two decades, including MTN's Project Fame, played a documented role in regional artist development outside Lagos, launching the initial careers of performers such as Chike, Iyanya, Niniola, and Johnny Drille, notes Brand Spur.
  • Coca-Cola partners with CAF for AFCON 2027 and 2028: Coca-Cola signed a sponsorship agreement in Johannesburg with the Confederation of African Football (CAF) for the 2027 tournament in Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda, as well as the 2028 edition, incorporating music and fan activations into its campaign, reports allAfrica.

Streaming and platforms

  • Moroccan indie distributor TarabX reports early traction: Casablanca-based distributor TarabX, founded in April 2026 by producer Younes Elachaby to serve Arabic-language independent artists, reached 2,098 registered acts, 168 paying subscribers, and 164 active releases, with monthly streams growing twentyfold between June and September, according to Hypebot.
  • Rema and BNXN lead Spotify Nigeria charts: Rema occupied the top four slots of the Spotify Nigeria daily chart on November 20 with tracks from his E-Mortal Boy EP, led by "Feel Alright" featuring Wizkid, before BNXN’s "If You Like" climbed back to No. 2 on November 21, reports The InterSpace Daily.
  • Audiomack schedules regional showcase in Nigeria: Audiomack and Evado announced a live concert scheduled for December 6 in Enugu, as noted by The Nation Newspaper.
  • Artist advocacy on African streaming payouts: Congolese-French artist Mokobé publicly questioned YouTube Music global head Lyor Cohen regarding the monetization levels and ad revenue distribution for African creators on the platform, via Mbote.

Live and festivals

  • Move Afrika to pilot USDC stablecoin payments in Rwanda: Global Citizen and fintech Circle will introduce USDC stablecoin payouts for local production vendors and technical contractors starting with their 2027 events in Rwanda, targeting faster settlement times and lower cross-border fees, reports allAfrica.
  • Lagos year-end concert schedule fills up: The city's December live calendar features heavy venue competition across Victoria Island and Ikoyi, with Flytime Fest booking Aṣa and Babyface for December 23, Darkoo headlining CURATE Lagos on December 14, and Nigerian Breweries organizing citywide festive showcases featuring Wizkid and Davido, according to Arise News and The InterSpace Daily.
  • South Africa's LYFE Festival combines brand activations and live sets: Broadcaster Y has scheduled its LYFE event for November 29 at Marks Park Sports Club in Johannesburg, featuring Sjava, Shekhinah, and Sun-El Musician alongside commercial partnerships with Captain Morgan and Tata, reports Music In Africa.
  • Cape Verde's Kontornu Festival faces funding doubts: Choreographer and organizer Djam Neguin warned that the fifth edition of the Kontornu festival, slated for May 2027 on Santiago island, may be cancelled by year-end without financial commitments from Cape Verde’s Ministry of Culture, according to Expresso das Ilhas.
  • International tour booking: American singer Jason Derulo is scheduled to perform at the Théâtre Royal in Rabat, Morocco, on December 4, as listed by Le Matin Maroc.

Rights and regulation

  • Industry leaders tackle AI and copyright in Johannesburg: The inaugural AI In Music Conference gathered 150 rights holders, lawyers, and policymakers on November 19 at SABC Radio Park. Organized by the Composers, Authors and Publishers Association (CAPASSO), the South African Music Performance Rights Association (SAMPRA), and the Academy of Sound Engineering, the meeting focused on legislative reforms to counter unauthorized training of generative AI on copyrighted catalogs, reports Music In Africa.
  • IFPI urges institutional reinforcement across Sub-Saharan Africa: Angela Ndambuki, Regional Director for Sub-Saharan Africa at the International Federation of the Phonographic Industry (IFPI), warned at CANEX WKND 2026 in Lagos that weak administrative capacity and slow statutory enforcement are preventing the local sector from turning global streaming visibility into actual royalty collections, reports Downtown Music Africa.
  • Sync licensing workshop targets South African creators: Producer and artist Thabo "Morale" Malefane led a publishing and synchronization workshop backed by CAPASSO at the South African Hip Hop Museum on November 19, focusing on catalog registration and placement agreements across TV, gaming, and advertising, according to Music In Africa.
  • Prerequisites for collective payouts in Uganda: The Uganda Performing Right Society (UPRS) reiterated that split agreements must be filed formally before royalty distributions can be paid to co-creators, notes InterSpace Music Distribution.

Other African news

  • Touré Kunda licensed for Grand Theft Auto VI: Veteran Senegalese group Touré Kunda will feature in Rockstar Games' upcoming Grand Theft Auto VI with their track "Nobel," placed on the fictional in-game station Kaleidoscope FM hosted by Jeremy Irons, reports Le Soleil.
  • Basketmouth releases second volume of Yabasi: Nigerian producer and comedian Basketmouth released the 10-track project Yabasi Vol. 2 through Barons World Music, featuring collaborations with Falz, Joeboy, and Johnny Drille, as reported by The InterSpace Daily.
  • Don Jazzy produces EU-backed advocacy single: Producer Don Jazzy previewed a campaign record featuring Yemi Alade, Chike, Qing Madi, and Ali Jita in partnership with the European Union delegation in Nigeria, according to The InterSpace Daily.
  • Namibia's Mr Makoya stages album launch: Former Matongo Family member Mr Makoya announced the free launch event for his fifth studio album Tycoon on November 27 at Windhoek's Escape Lounge, featuring collaborators T-Bozz & Staika and Adora, reports The Namibian.

Key takeaways

  • Audit catalog agreements and publishing splits: The legal clash between Fathermoh and Black Market Records demonstrates the risk of signing standard agreements without explicit audit, royalty accounting, and catalog reversion clauses.
  • Target international streaming listeners to lift payout yields: Data from TAG and Livespot360 shows that Nigerian artist revenues can increase significantly by optimizing playlisting and marketing in higher-paying foreign markets, even without raw streaming volume expansion.
  • Explore alternative payment rails for cross-border live events: Promoters handling international acts and local stage crews should watch the Global Citizen and Circle USDC rollout in Rwanda, which may offer practical solutions to bypass costly foreign exchange hurdles.
