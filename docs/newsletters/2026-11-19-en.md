# SAMRO's Digital Surge and Makerverse's Pan-African Royalty Bet

## Editorial
The gap between Africa's consumption growth and actual royalty payouts remains the central bottleneck for the continent's music business. This week, SAMRO posted record collection numbers largely driven by streaming and video settlements, even as direct licensing and cross-border collections remain complex for independent rights holders. Meanwhile, new infrastructure plays like Makerverse are attempting to tackle the payment pipeline across 20 markets. For artists and executives alike, the focus is shifting from simply getting heard to fixing back-end administration.

## Top stories
**SAMRO licensing revenue reaches 769.3 million South African rands ($44.6 million)**
The Southern African Music Rights Organisation (SAMRO), the collective management organization representing music rights holders in Southern Africa, reported a 13.1% rise in licensing revenue for the fiscal year ended June 30, 2026, reaching 769.3 million South African rands (approximately $44.6 million). Royalty distributions to members climbed 15.6% to 485.3 million rands, with an additional 708.3 million rands scheduled for distribution in FY2027. Digital licensing drove the performance with a 61.6% year-on-year increase, partly lifted by non-recurring settlements with streaming and video-on-demand services, as reported by [Music In Africa](https://musicinafrica.net/magazine/samro-reports-13-1-increase-in-licensing-revenue-to-44-6m/).

**Makerverse rolls out local-currency distribution across 20 African nations**
Cape Town-based music tech firm Makerverse has introduced a pan-African digital distribution and payout platform operating across 20 countries, according to reports by [The InterSpace Daily](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/makerverse-launches-african-music-distribution-and-royalty-platform/) and [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/africas-music-industry-is-booming-but-how-do-artists-get-paid/). The system promises earnings disbursed in local currencies within two days of availability and sets a minimum withdrawal threshold of $10. Citing an internal survey of 2,000 artists that identified roughly $1 million in unclaimed royalties, the company aims to eliminate intermediary foreign exchange losses that frequently eat up over 10% of creator income.

**Lagos positions Detty December for electronic music with Black Coffee and This Is Me**
Promoters M:E Entertainment and DAPO will host This Is Me, a new 12-hour electronic music festival expecting 5,000 attendees, in Lagos on December 19 with South African DJ Black Coffee as headliner, reports [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/lagos-has-eyes-on-becoming-the-next-electronic-music-capital/) and [The InterSpace Daily](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/lagos-festival-this-is-me-books-black-coffee-for-december-19/). The move reflects an expanding local live production capacity that relies less on imported production hardware, extending the city's live footprint beyond traditional Afrobeats events.

## Rights and regulation
**ARIPO, WIPO, and URSB host regional copyright symposium in Kampala**
To mark its 50th anniversary, the African Regional Intellectual Property Organization (ARIPO) partnered with the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) and the Uganda Registration Services Bureau (URSB) to organize a copyright management symposium in Kampala, reports [Adams & Adams IP Africa](https://www.adams.africa/adams-news/aripo-50s-symposium-on-copyright-and-related-rights/). Discussions centered on cross-border enforcement, aligning national registries, and reforming licensing mechanisms across member states.

**Uganda prepares to gazette updated copyright regulations**
Ugandan authorities are finalizing the formal gazettement of fresh copyright regulations to strengthen local enforcement and administrative standards, according to [Daily Monitor Uganda](https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/new-copyright-regulations-set-for-gazettement-5675192).

**NCC and WIPO review Nigerian copyright registration infrastructure**
The Nigerian Copyright Commission (NCC) and WIPO are reviewing options to upgrade Nigeria's existing online copyright registration platform, reports [The InterSpace Daily](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/nigerian-copyright-commission-wipo-registration-upgrade-assessment/). The current system remains functional while assessment work continues.

**Demystifying registration vs. royalty collection in Nigeria and Namibia**
Legal analyses published this week highlight common compliance gaps for African creators. [The InterSpace Daily](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/nigeria-copyright-registration-royalty-collection-checklist/) notes that securing an NCC copyright certificate merely establishes ownership of a work and does not collect performance royalties, which requires affiliation with approved collective management organizations (CMOs) alongside precise split sheets. Similarly, [The Namibian](https://www.namibian.com.na/why-namibian-artists-must-master-direct-licensing/) emphasizes that while local CMO Nascam issues blanket public performance licences, rights holders must handle direct sync licensing themselves to monetize television and commercial placements.

## Key takeaways
- **Audit catalog documentation beyond CMO registration:** An institutional copyright certificate or collective society membership does not automatically monetize every use. Rights holders must maintain distinct master, publishing split, and cue sheets to claim broadcasting and sync royalties across Southern and West Africa.
- **Factor one-off settlements out of digital royalty projections:** SAMRO's 61.6% digital revenue spike shows progress, but the organization notes that retroactive streaming payouts represent non-recurring income. Publishers and labels should avoid modeling future cash flow on these one-off windfalls.
- **Evaluate local-currency distribution terms:** The arrival of platforms like Makerverse offering direct payouts in local African currencies may reduce banking intermediary deductions, but rights holders must review specific payout timelines and reporting detail before migrating primary distribution pipelines.
