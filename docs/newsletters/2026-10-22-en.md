# Building Sustainable African Infrastructure

## Editorial
African industry convening platforms are setting the agenda this week, with major industry events like CANEX WKND and ACCES detailing their roadmaps for continental trade and live development. Simultaneously, independent infrastructure is evolving globally, exemplified by EMPIRE founder Ghazi launching a dedicated B2B distribution and services platform. Streaming margins continue to tighten as major DSPs aggressively crack down on account sharing to maximize revenue per user. For African creators and executives, the path forward relies less on algorithmic chance and far more on building owned audiences, clear rights frameworks, and sustainable local live ecosystems.

## Top stories
**CANEX WKND and ACCES lock in 2026 programs**  
Two major continental music and creative industry gatherings have confirmed their 2026 line-ups and strategic discussions. [Music In Africa](https://musicinafrica.net/magazine/nigeria-canex-wknd-unveils-2026-programme/) reported that CANEX WKND rolled out its programming, backed by discussions with Afreximbank leadership on cultural trade. Concurrently, pan-African music conference [ACCES announced its 2026 program](https://musicinafrica.net/magazine/acces-unveils-stellar-programme-for-2026-edition/), emphasizing professional networking, technical workshops, and cross-border live showcases.

**EMPIRE founder Ghazi unveils B2B company Supply Chain**  
EMPIRE founder Ghazi has launched Supply Chain, an independent B2B music infrastructure firm aimed at global distribution and back-end support, as reported by [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/empire-founder-ghazi-launches-b2b-music-infrastructure-company-supply-chain-globally-with-beatport-owned-ampsuite-as-launch-partner/). The company has teamed up with Beatport-owned Ampsuite as a launch partner and brought in Craig May as SVP of Marketing and Kristy May as SVP of Business Affairs. The venture offers an alternative technical backbone for labels navigating global scale.

**Spotify adds 6 million subscribers as profits climb**  
Spotify posted its Q3 2026 financial results, adding 6 million net subscribers and placing the platform on track for a 3.3 billion dollar annual operating profit, according to [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-is-on-course-for-a-3-3b-annual-operating-profit-in-2026-after-adding-6m-subscribers-in-q3/). Alongside its balance sheet gains, the streaming service has initiated a stricter crackdown on Family plan sharing, barring users who fail residential checks from joining shared plans for 12 months.

- **SoundExchange lawsuit paused:** A 3.4 million dollar lawsuit filed by SoundExchange against hardware and audio provider Sonos has been paused by the courts while related appeals on statutory rights-to-sue unfold, reported by [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/soundexchanges-3-4m-lawsuit-against-sonos-put-on-hold-until-appeals-court-rules-on-its-right-to-sue-over-unpaid-royalties/).

## Key takeaways
1. **Engage directly with trade markets:** With CANEX WKND and ACCES unveiling their formal programs, managers and label executives should target these gatherings for business development and sync pitching rather than treating them merely as live performance dates.
2. **Audit multi-user plan impacts:** Spotify is actively auditing account sharing across Duo and Family subscriptions. Expect higher friction for low-income subscribers in African territories and adjust direct-to-consumer and physical merchandise plans to balance potential ARPU changes.
3. **Seek corporate cultural sponsorship:** One NICO's K250m injection into Malawi's Mulhako Festival shows that institutional finance brands are increasingly viewing regional heritage and music gatherings as strategic marketing vehicles. Festival organizers should package their proposals around formal economic impact rather than general entertainment.
