# Legal Battles, Live Infrastructure, and the Economic Engine of African Music

### Editorial

This week highlights the gap between Africa’s creative reach and its underlying business framework. While Lagos prepares for another massive festive concert season and independent tech platforms emerge in North Africa, critical issues remain around contracts and royalty administration. In Kenya, a 87.6 million Kenyan shilling legal dispute exposes the ongoing friction between independent talent and label agreements. For executives and creators alike, building sustainable infrastructure, from local copyright enforcement to cross-border live payments, remains the central challenge.

---

### Top stories

**Fathermoh sues Black Market Records for 87.6 million Kenyan shillings**  
Kenyan Gengetone artist Moses Otieno, known as Fathermoh, has taken US-based Black Market Records to court, seeking 87.6 million Kenyan shillings (approximately $675,000) over alleged withheld royalties, catalog access restrictions, and unauthorized exploitation. Court filings show the 2021 agreement assigned production, marketing, and distribution to the label in exchange for 20% of royalties and 50% of publishing rights. This dispute follows a broader legal injunction reported by [The EastAfrican](https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/magazine/us-music-label-barred-from-exploiting-kenyan-artistes-music--5684000), which barred the label from exploiting several Kenyan artists' catalogs, as detailed by [Business Today Kenya](https://businesstoday.co.ke/who-owns-fathermohs-music-inside-the-ksh87-million-dispute-that-could-change-his-career/).

**Lagos creative economy: Nightlife hits $2 billion as venue gaps persist**  
A new report titled *Off the Charts: The Afrobeats Export Economy*, published by TAG by Oui Capital and Livespot360, values the Lagos nightlife economy at $2 billion in 2025, projecting it could reach $2.8 billion by 2027 with additional venues and recurring events. The study notes that Nigerian artists' Spotify payouts could climb from $39.4 million to $99.4 million if the share of international listeners increases, even without rising total streams, according to [Vanguard News](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/11/afrobeats-lagos-nightlife-economy-hits-2bn-report-identifies-five-investment-opportunities/).

**Live Nation signals expansion in Nigeria after Dave's record Lagos dates**  
British-Nigerian rapper Dave drew over 4,000 attendees across two nights at the newly refurbished ₦68 billion (€42 million) Wole Soyinka Centre for Culture and the Creative Arts in October, setting an attendance record for a single act at the venue. Promoted by Live Nation alongside local firm MASSIVE, the tour marks Live Nation's growing operational focus on West Africa ahead of the opening of the planned 12,000-capacity Lagos Arena, reports [IQ Magazine](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/11/daves-lagos-shows-a-great-sign-of-whats-possible-in-nigeria/).

### Business and sponsorship

* **Aristokrat Records CEO reflects on Burna Boy renewal terms:** Peedi Picasso, CEO of Aristokrat Records, revealed he once rejected an 85-15% revenue split demanded by Burna Boy during contract renewal talks, following an earlier 60-40 split in the singer's favor. Picasso stated that the economics of the Nigerian market at the time, which relied heavily on live dates and endorsements rather than recorded sales, made the demand unviable, as reported by [Blueprint Newspapers](https://blueprint.ng/how-disagreement-with-burna-boy-started-record-label-boss/).
* **Chocolate City combines physical scouting with AI in A&R:** Osagie Onobun, head of A&R at Nigerian label Chocolate City, explained that alongside physical university tours in cities like Enugu and Ile-Ife, the company now systematically integrates digital discovery apps, artificial intelligence searches, and streaming audience analytics to sign new talent, according to [Business News Nigeria](https://businessday.ng/life-arts/article/nigerian-music-labels-blend-traditional-ar-with-data-as-talent-discovery-changes/).
* **Corporate talent programs and Afrobeats development:** Sustained corporate competitions over the past two decades, including MTN's Project Fame, played a documented role in regional artist development outside Lagos, launching the initial careers of performers such as Chike, Iyanya, Niniola, and Johnny Drille, notes [Brand Spur](https://brandspurng.com/2026/11/23/mtns-20-year-talent-investment-helped-turn-regional-music-stars-into-afrobeats-icons/).
* **Coca-Cola partners with CAF for AFCON 2027 and 2028:** Coca-Cola signed a sponsorship agreement in Johannesburg with the Confederation of African Football (CAF) for the 2027 tournament in Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda, as well as the 2028 edition, incorporating music and fan activations into its campaign, reports [allAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202611240251.html).

### Rights and regulation

* **Industry leaders tackle AI and copyright in Johannesburg:** The inaugural AI In Music Conference gathered 150 rights holders, lawyers, and policymakers on November 19 at SABC Radio Park. Organized by the Composers, Authors and Publishers Association (CAPASSO), the South African Music Performance Rights Association (SAMPRA), and the Academy of Sound Engineering, the meeting focused on legislative reforms to counter unauthorized training of generative AI on copyrighted catalogs, reports [Music In Africa](https://musicinafrica.net/magazine/music-industry-stakeholders-discuss-ai-regulation-copyright-protection-at-joburg-conference/).
* **IFPI urges institutional reinforcement across Sub-Saharan Africa:** Angela Ndambuki, Regional Director for Sub-Saharan Africa at the International Federation of the Phonographic Industry (IFPI), warned at CANEX WKND 2026 in Lagos that weak administrative capacity and slow statutory enforcement are preventing the local sector from turning global streaming visibility into actual royalty collections, reports [Downtown Music Africa](https://downtownmusic.africa/ifpi-calls-on-africa-to-strengthen-music-copyright-protection-and-royalty-collection/).
* **Sync licensing workshop targets South African creators:** Producer and artist Thabo "Morale" Malefane led a publishing and synchronization workshop backed by CAPASSO at the South African Hip Hop Museum on November 19, focusing on catalog registration and placement agreements across TV, gaming, and advertising, according to [Music In Africa](https://musicinafrica.net/magazine/sa-music-industry-figures-discuss-publishing-and-royalties-at-sync-money-academy/).
* **Prerequisites for collective payouts in Uganda:** The Uganda Performing Right Society (UPRS) reiterated that split agreements must be filed formally before royalty distributions can be paid to co-creators, notes [InterSpace Music Distribution](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/uganda-music-royalties-uprs-split-agreement-payout/).

### Key takeaways

* **Audit catalog agreements and publishing splits:** The legal clash between Fathermoh and Black Market Records demonstrates the risk of signing standard agreements without explicit audit, royalty accounting, and catalog reversion clauses.
* **Target international streaming listeners to lift payout yields:** Data from TAG and Livespot360 shows that Nigerian artist revenues can increase significantly by optimizing playlisting and marketing in higher-paying foreign markets, even without raw streaming volume expansion.
* **Explore alternative payment rails for cross-border live events:** Promoters handling international acts and local stage crews should watch the Global Citizen and Circle USDC rollout in Rwanda, which may offer practical solutions to bypass costly foreign exchange hurdles.
