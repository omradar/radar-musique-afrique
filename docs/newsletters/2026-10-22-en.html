B2B Infrastructure, Festival Capital, and Regional Touring: African Music Industry Brief

Editorial

African music continues to test its scale beyond local borders, from Bien's international positioning to the return of continent-wide trade summits like ACCES and CANEX WKND. At the same time, global distribution rails are being rebuilt as major players launch dedicated business-to-business infrastructure. The core priority for operators across the continent remains execution: locking down corporate sponsorships, passing long-delayed artist status legislation, and organizing viable live circuits within domestic markets.

Top stories

EMPIRE founder Ghazi announced the global launch of Supply Chain, a B2B music infrastructure company partnered with Beatport-owned Ampsuite. Reported by Music Business Worldwide, the venture targets backend delivery and distribution logistics for music companies, hiring Craig May and Kristy May as senior vice presidents for marketing and business affairs.

Kenyan vocalist Bien-Aimé Baraza is preparing the release of Alusa Continua, a 17-track project featuring international collaborations. According to Business Today Kenya, the upcoming record serves as a deliberate strategy to expand East African pop beyond the regional market.

Pan-African industry conferences ACCES and CANEX WKND have both published their 2026 programming. Coverage from Music In Africa and an interview series on Music In Africa show both events focusing heavily on creative export, cross-border funding, and infrastructure development.

Business and sponsorship

Corporate backing provided a boost to regional culture in Malawi, where financial group One NICO committed K250m to the Mulhako Cultural Festival, as reported by allAfrica (EN).

PR executive Liz Morentin, previously Senior Vice President and Head of Global Communications at Virgin Music Group, launched Greenvalley Media & Marketing to service music and entertainment brands, according to Music Business Worldwide.

In a research note published by MIDiA Research, the company outlines how established digital creators are transitioning into capitalized media operators, directly funding and mentoring early-stage talent.

Streaming and platforms

Spotify posted its Q3 2026 performance, adding 6 million subscribers during the quarter. According to Music Business Worldwide, the platform is now tracking toward a $3.3 billion annual operating profit.

Spotify is also tightening account restrictions on its Family plans. As reported by Music Business Worldwide, users who fail location verification within seven days are barred from joining another Family or Duo plan for 12 months.

In Senegal, domestic audio engineering platform Senmixmaster claims 1,500 users, turning to automated mixing tools to lower production barriers for local artists, according to allAfrica (FR).

Live and festivals

Live ticketing and marketing technology provider Hive closed a $27 million Series B round led by Frontier Growth, according to Music Business Worldwide. The company works with over 1,600 venues and promoters globally.

Senegalese singer Coumba Gawlo met with Niger's Head of State General Abdourahamane Tiani in Niamey, where an all-female vocal festival was announced for January 2027, reports allAfrica (FR).

In Senegal, veteran group Orchestra Baobab unveiled plans for a national regional tour, detailed by allAfrica (FR), bringing live performances outside the capital.

In Côte d'Ivoire, industry professionals held panel discussions regarding structural hurdles in breaking domestic talent abroad and organizing sustainable live stages at home, per allAfrica (FR).

Rights and regulation

In Mauritius, artist and advocate Joëlle Coret urged the government to prioritize the "Status of the Artist Act" to establish formal social, legal, and economic protections for cultural workers, per allAfrica (FR).

In the United States, SoundExchange's $3.4 million lawsuit against Sonos over unpaid royalties remains stayed, reports Music Business Worldwide, pending a federal appellate decision on its right to sue.

Key takeaways

  1. Anticipate platform account tightening: Spotify's 12-month lock on unauthorized Family account sharing will impact streaming metrics in emerging markets where shared plans are widespread. Marketing plans must prepare for possible shifts in reported user bases.
  2. Track the emergence of independent B2B rails: Ghazi's launch of Supply Chain signals that catalog owners and labels can increasingly bypass legacy systems for custom distribution and operations backend support.
  3. Build regional touring outside primary hubs: Orchestra Baobab's regional dates in Senegal highlight the recurring business need to develop touring circuits beyond major commercial capitals like Dakar, Abidjan, or Lagos.
