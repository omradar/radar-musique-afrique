Building Sustainable African Infrastructure

Editorial

African industry convening platforms are setting the agenda this week, with major industry events like CANEX WKND and ACCES detailing their roadmaps for continental trade and live development. Simultaneously, independent infrastructure is evolving globally, exemplified by EMPIRE founder Ghazi launching a dedicated B2B distribution and services platform. Streaming margins continue to tighten as major DSPs aggressively crack down on account sharing to maximize revenue per user. For African creators and executives, the path forward relies less on algorithmic chance and far more on building owned audiences, clear rights frameworks, and sustainable local live ecosystems.

Top stories

CANEX WKND and ACCES lock in 2026 programs
Two major continental music and creative industry gatherings have confirmed their 2026 line-ups and strategic discussions. Music In Africa reported that CANEX WKND rolled out its programming, backed by discussions with Afreximbank leadership on cultural trade. Concurrently, pan-African music conference ACCES announced its 2026 program, emphasizing professional networking, technical workshops, and cross-border live showcases.

EMPIRE founder Ghazi unveils B2B company Supply Chain
EMPIRE founder Ghazi has launched Supply Chain, an independent B2B music infrastructure firm aimed at global distribution and back-end support, as reported by Music Business Worldwide. The company has teamed up with Beatport-owned Ampsuite as a launch partner and brought in Craig May as SVP of Marketing and Kristy May as SVP of Business Affairs. The venture offers an alternative technical backbone for labels navigating global scale.

Spotify adds 6 million subscribers as profits climb
Spotify posted its Q3 2026 financial results, adding 6 million net subscribers and placing the platform on track for a 3.3 billion dollar annual operating profit, according to Music Business Worldwide. Alongside its balance sheet gains, the streaming service has initiated a stricter crackdown on Family plan sharing, barring users who fail residential checks from joining shared plans for 12 months.

Business and sponsorship

  • Corporate capital for Malawian live culture: Financial services company One NICO committed 250 million Malawian kwacha to the Mulhako Festival, positioning cultural events as drivers of economic development, as reported by allAfrica (EN).
  • Executive PR launch: Former Virgin Music Group global communications head Liz Morentin has formed Greenvalley Media & Marketing, targeting brand strategy and narrative building across independent music, covered by Music Business Worldwide.
  • R&B crossover push: R&B collective Both Worlds has formally set sights on expanding into the South African territory, detailed by Music In Africa.
  • AI production adoption: In Senegal, local mastering platform Senmixmaster claims a user base of 1,500 producers while using artificial intelligence to handle audio finishing, according to allAfrica (FR).

Streaming and platforms

  • Live marketing tech capital: Ticketing and marketing software firm Hive raised a 27 million dollar Series B round led by Frontier Growth, reported by Music Business Worldwide. The company states it serves over 1,600 venues and festivals and has supported 1 billion dollars in ticket sales.
  • In-car listening: Following the retirement of its dedicated Car Thing hardware, Spotify introduced an integrated in-car application with Audi, covered by Music Business Worldwide.
  • Creator business evolution: In research published by the firm, MIDiA Research outlined how top creators are increasingly functioning as venture-backed media enterprises, investing direct capital into smaller creative teams.

Live and festivals

  • Sahel cultural diplomacy: Senegalese vocalist Coumba Gawlo met with General Abdourahamane Tiani in Niamey, where plans were announced for a women's vocal festival to be held in Niger in January 2027, according to allAfrica (FR).
  • Touring regional circuits: Orchestra Baobab announced an upcoming national tour across Senegal's regional hubs, covered by allAfrica (FR).
  • Ivorian export debate: Industry stakeholders held panel sessions examining the lack of structural support and tour packaging needed to export newer Ivorian musical scenes beyond West Africa, reported by allAfrica (FR).

Rights and regulation

  • Mauritius artist protections: Mauritian cultural advocate Joëlle Coret urged the government to implement the long-delayed "Status of the Artist Act" to formalize social security and professional legal protections for musicians, as covered by allAfrica (FR).
  • SoundExchange lawsuit paused: A 3.4 million dollar lawsuit filed by SoundExchange against hardware and audio provider Sonos has been paused by the courts while related appeals on statutory rights-to-sue unfold, reported by Music Business Worldwide.

Key takeaways

  1. Engage directly with trade markets: With CANEX WKND and ACCES unveiling their formal programs, managers and label executives should target these gatherings for business development and sync pitching rather than treating them merely as live performance dates.
  2. Audit multi-user plan impacts: Spotify is actively auditing account sharing across Duo and Family subscriptions. Expect higher friction for low-income subscribers in African territories and adjust direct-to-consumer and physical merchandise plans to balance potential ARPU changes.
  3. Seek corporate cultural sponsorship: One NICO's K250m injection into Malawi's Mulhako Festival shows that institutional finance brands are increasingly viewing regional heritage and music gatherings as strategic marketing vehicles. Festival organizers should package their proposals around formal economic impact rather than general entertainment.
